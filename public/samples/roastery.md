## The room

![A long wooden table in a bright café, a single cup of coffee and a phone left on it](https://picsum.photos/id/42/1200/900)

We roast in the back and pour in the front, and there is one long table in between. It was a workbench in a boatyard before it was ours, and it still has the vice marks to prove it.

People ask why there are no small tables. The answer is that strangers who share a table for long enough stop being strangers, and that has always seemed like the point of a café.

## On the bar this season

### Finca La Palma

Washed Caturra from Huila, Colombia. Red apple, brown sugar and a long, clean finish. Our house espresso until spring.

### Guji Highland

Natural heirloom from Ethiopia. Blueberry, jasmine and something close to dark chocolate as it cools.

### Kayon Mountain

Honey process, also Ethiopian. Apricot and black tea. Best as a slow filter on a grey morning.

### Sitio Boa Vista

Pulped natural from Minas Gerais, Brazil. Hazelnut and milk chocolate. The one we reach for with milk.

## The winter roast

For the cold months we stretch the roast by a minute and drop the beans a little later. It trades some brightness for sweetness and body — the difference between a crisp apple and a baked one.

| Minute | Bean temperature (°C) |

> You cannot roast flavour into a coffee. You can only decide how much of what the farmer grew you are willing to burn away.

> — Marta, head roaster

## How we brew filter at home

1. Weigh 15 g of coffee and grind it like coarse sand
2. Rinse the paper and warm the cup
3. Pour 50 g of water at 94 °C and wait 30 seconds
4. Pour slowly to 250 g, in circles
5. Let it drain; it should take about three minutes
