# Four slow days in Kyoto

![Rows of vermilion torii gates on a hillside path](https://picsum.photos/seed/kyoto-gates/2400/1350)

An unhurried itinerary for late autumn, built around long walks, small meals and getting up before everyone else.

## Go early

The city belongs to whoever is awake at six.

## Fushimi Inari at dawn

![A stone path climbing through a forest in morning light](https://picsum.photos/seed/inari-dawn/1200/1500)

Take the first train on the Nara line and you will have the lower gates almost to yourself. The climb to the summit takes about ninety minutes at an easy pace, and the crowds thin with every shrine you pass.

Bring water and a few coins. Half way up there is a tea house with a view across the southern city where you can sit as long as you like for the price of a bowl of udon.

## The Philosopher's Path

![A canal lined with maple trees in red and gold](https://picsum.photos/seed/kyoto-canal/2200/1300)

Two kilometres of canal under maples, from Ginkaku-ji to Nanzen-ji. In the last week of November the water runs red with fallen leaves.

## What to eat

### Yudofu

Silken tofu simmered in kelp broth, eaten in temple gardens around Nanzen-ji. Order the set and take your time.

### Obanzai

Kyoto home cooking: a dozen small seasonal dishes on the counter. Point at what looks good.

### Nishin soba

Buckwheat noodles with sweet simmered herring, a dish that makes sense only once the weather turns cold.

### Wagashi

Sweets shaped for the season — maple leaves in November — served with whisked matcha.

## Before you go

- Buy an IC card at the station
- Carry cash for temples
- Book dinner two days ahead
- Pack shoes that slip off easily
- Learn to say thank you: *arigatō gozaimasu*
