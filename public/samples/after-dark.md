# After dark

![A suspension bridge and a lit skyline reflected in purple water at night](https://picsum.photos/id/249/2400/1350)

A field guide to photographing the city once the sun has gone — what to carry, where to stand, and how long to hold your breath.

## The city changes shifts

Around nine the offices empty and the lights take over.

## Start at blue hour

![A lone figure in silhouette on a ridge under a deep blue and orange dusk sky](https://picsum.photos/id/505/1200/900)

The best night photographs are not taken at night. They are taken in the twenty minutes after sunset, when the sky still holds enough light to separate rooftops from the dark, and the street lamps have just come on.

Arrive early. Find your frame while you can still see your feet, then wait. The light will come to you, and it will not stay long.

## How fast the light goes

Measured on a clear evening from a rooftop, in lux. Your eyes adapt so smoothly that you will not notice the drop; your camera will.

| Minutes after sunset | Light level (lux) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 400 |
| 5 | 210 |
| 10 | 110 |
| 15 | 55 |
| 20 | 26 |
| 25 | 12 |

## Let the traffic draw

![Red and white light trails from traffic sweeping around a bend in the road](https://picsum.photos/id/352/2400/1400)

Open the shutter for ten seconds and every car becomes a brushstroke. Red going away, white coming toward you.

## Four ways to make a picture

### Go long

Put the camera on something solid and leave the shutter open. Water turns to glass, crowds dissolve, and headlights write their own lines.

### Go wide open

Use your fastest lens at its widest aperture. Distant lamps melt into soft discs and a single sharp subject stands in front of them.

### Look down

After rain every pavement is a mirror. Crouch, tilt, and let the puddle hold the neon the right way up.

### Stand still

Pick a lit doorway and wait for someone to walk through it. The light is the stage; the passer-by is the performance.

## Find the light, then the subject

![A narrow market lane at night under rows of string lights, shop fronts glowing on either side](https://picsum.photos/id/195/768/1024)

Beginners look for something to photograph and then hope the light is good. At night, reverse it. Walk until you find a pool of light — a shop window, a row of bulbs, a single lamp over a doorway — and build the picture around it.

String lights are generous. They give you depth, they flatter faces, and they tell the viewer exactly how the evening felt.

## Exposure cheat sheet

Starting points, not rules. Take one frame, look at it, and adjust.

| Scene | ISO | Aperture | Shutter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blue hour skyline | 100 | f/8 | 4 s |
| Light trails | 100 | f/11 | 15 s |
| Lit street, handheld | 3200 | f/1.8 | 1/60 s |
| Neon signs | 400 | f/4 | 1/125 s |
| Rain on a window | 1600 | f/2 | 1/100 s |
| Stars outside the city | 3200 | f/2.8 | 20 s |

## Then leave the city

![The Milky Way faintly visible in a dark green sky above a wooden fence](https://picsum.photos/id/120/2400/1400)

Drive an hour past the last street lamp and look up. Twenty seconds, wide open, and the sky fills in.

## What to carry

- A tripod you trust
- One fast prime lens
- A spare battery, kept warm
- A torch with a red filter
- Gloves you can shoot in

## One evening, hour by hour

| When | What to shoot |
| --- | --- |
| Sunset | Scout the frame and set up the tripod |
| +20 min | Blue hour: skylines, bridges, rooftops |
| +45 min | Light trails from a footbridge |
| +90 min | Shop windows, markets, people in doorways |
| Late | Empty streets, rain, reflections |

## A parting thought

> The night is not the absence of light. It is light, edited.
